SiteOps · Release Check

Test Walkthrough

A screen-by-screen check of everything that changed. No technical knowledge needed — just follow the steps and tell us what looks wrong.

0 of 31 checked

Before you start

A large batch of fixes went into SiteOps and none of it has been looked at by a person yet. Your job is to use the app normally and flag anything that looks broken, ugly, or confusing.

  • Use a phone if you can. Most of this app is used on site, on a phone. A laptop is fine as a backup but some checks are about how it feels on a small screen.
  • You need an Admin login. Some screens only appear for Admins. If you can't see a screen mentioned here, you may be signed in as a Supervisor — that's worth telling us either way.
  • The app is dark by design. Dark background, light text, everywhere. That's intentional — not a bug.
  • Use real-looking data, but treat it as real. This is the live system with real client information. Please don't delete anything you didn't create yourself.

Tick each box as you go — your progress is saved on this device. If something fails, note the number and what you saw.

Getting around

The bar at the bottom and the strip at the top of every screen.

01

The bottom bar works

Do this

  1. Sign in and look at the bar fixed to the bottom of the screen.
  2. Tap each item in turn: Home, Requests, the big + button in the middle, Transfers, and Admin (or Profile).

What should happen

Each one takes you somewhere and the icon for the screen you're on is highlighted in blue.

Every icon is a clean drawing. None of them are blank boxes, question marks, or stray letters.

All the icons in the app were swapped to a new set, so a missing icon is the most likely thing to have gone wrong.

02

The buttons at the top are easy to hit

Do this

  1. On any screen, look at the top strip: a back arrow on the left, a search icon and a bell on the right.
  2. Tap each one with your thumb, not your fingertip. Try it as if you were wearing gloves.

What should happen

Each button is comfortably big — you shouldn't have to aim carefully or tap twice.

If you have unread alerts, the small dot sits neatly on the corner of the bell, not floating away from it or overlapping the middle of it.

These were made bigger on purpose. The dot had to move to match.

03

The header doesn't get squashed

Do this

  1. Open a screen with a long title — a site name, or a category page.
  2. Look closely at the top strip on the narrowest phone you have.

What should happen

The title and the buttons sit on one tidy row.

Flag it if anything wraps onto a second line, overlaps, or gets pushed off the edge of the screen.

04

Back doesn't throw you out of the app

Do this

  1. Open a deep screen — a site, then one of its categories.
  2. Copy that page's web address.
  3. Open a brand new tab (or paste the link into a message and open it from there) so you arrive with no history behind you.
  4. Press the back arrow at the top left.

What should happen

You go up one level inside the app — to the site, or to the dashboard.

Flag it if back leaves SiteOps entirely, lands on a blank page, or takes you to a screen that has nothing to do with where you were.

This used to dump people out of the app when they arrived from a shared link or a notification.

Adding a log entry

The + button in the middle of the bottom bar. This is the most-used part of the app and the most heavily changed.

05

Choosing a category

Do this

  1. Tap the + button.
  2. Look at the list of categories.

What should happen

Every category has a small icon next to it and the icons all look like they belong to the same set.

Flag it if you see the word "category" written out as text where a picture should be, or an empty gap.

06

Error messages actually tell you what's wrong

Do this

  1. Start a new log entry of any type.
  2. Deliberately leave a required box empty — or type a silly number, like a negative wage.
  3. Press Save.

What should happen

A message appears naming the actual problem — something like "Enter a wage per head" or "Number of people must be at least 1".

Flag it if you get a vague message like "Something went wrong" or "An error occurred". That is exactly the bug that was supposed to be fixed.

07

Number boxes bring up the number keypad

Do this

  1. On a phone, start a new log entry.
  2. Tap into a box that takes a number — quantity, wage, number of people, an amount.

What should happen

The phone shows a number keypad, not the full letter keyboard.

Site staff enter a lot of numbers with one thumb. Do this on every numeric box you can find.

08

Labels are tappable

Do this

  1. In any form, tap the small text label above a box — the word "Quantity" itself, not the box.

What should happen

The box below it becomes active, ready for you to type.

09

Save one of each kind of entry

Do this

  1. Create and save one entry of each type: Labour, Materials, Machinery, an Expense, and an Incident.
  2. After each save, find it in the site's list and check the details are what you typed.

What should happen

All five save without error, and the numbers and dates match what you entered — especially the date.

The code behind all five types was rebuilt into one shared system. If one type got wired up wrong, this is where it shows.

10

Split labour adds up

Do this

  1. Add a Labour entry that has both masons and helpers, with different rates.
  2. Note the total you'd expect on paper.
  3. Save it, then look at the total shown in the list.

What should happen

The total matches your arithmetic exactly — to the rupee.

This sum was previously calculated in five different places in the code. They were merged into one. A mismatch here matters more than almost anything else in this list.

11

Units are a proper dropdown

Do this

  1. Add a Materials entry and open the Unit selector.
  2. Then go to Transfers in the bottom bar and open the Unit selector there too.

What should happen

Both show a list of real units you pick from — bags, kg, cubic metres and so on. Both lists offer the same units.

Flag it if the Transfers screen asks you to type something called a "Unit UUID" into a plain box. That was the old broken version and it should be gone.

12

Editing an existing entry

Do this

  1. Open an entry you created earlier and press Save without changing anything.
  2. Reopen it.
  3. Now change one value, save, and reopen again.

What should happen

Nothing changes on the first save. On the second, only the value you edited is different — the unit, date and everything else stay put.

13

Deleting asks first

Do this

  1. Delete an entry you created during this test.
  2. When asked to confirm, first press Cancel. Then try again and confirm.

What should happen

The confirmation is a styled panel that matches the app. Cancel leaves the entry alone. Confirming removes it, and it stays gone after you reload the page.

Flag it if you see a plain grey browser pop-up box — the kind with a bare "OK / Cancel" and no styling. Those were all supposed to be replaced.

14

Closed sites are read-only

Do this

  1. Find a site that is archived or completed.
  2. Try to add a new entry to it.

What should happen

You're stopped, with a message explaining why — that the site is closed to new entries.

Flag it if the entry saves anyway.

Looking at entries

Open a site from Home, then open one of its categories.

15

The list loads and shows the right amounts

Do this

  1. Open a busy site and look at its list of entries.
  2. Pick a few rows and check the amount shown against the entry itself.

What should happen

Rows appear quickly, each showing its date, description and amount. Nothing shows a blank where a number should be, and no row says "undefined" or "NaN".

The app now fetches only the columns it needs instead of everything. A missed column shows up as an empty space.

16

"Load more" on a long list

Do this

  1. Find a site with a lot of entries of one type — more than a couple of hundred if you can.
  2. Scroll to the bottom and press Load more.
  3. Press it again. Look carefully at the rows on either side of each join.

What should happen

More rows are added below the ones already there. No row appears twice, and there's no obvious gap in the dates where entries have been skipped.

17

Deleting from the list

Do this

  1. In the all-entries view, delete a row you created during this test.
  2. Watch the screen closely as it happens.

What should happen

The row disappears straight away, and the rest of the page stays still — no flicker, no full reload, no scroll position jumping.

18

Totals agree with each other

Do this

  1. On a site's main page, note the labour spend total.
  2. Open the labour entries list for the same site and add the individual amounts up.
  3. Search for the same site and check the figure shown in the search results too.

What should happen

All three figures are the same number.

These three totals used to be worked out separately. If they disagree, that's a real accounting problem and worth reporting immediately.

The catalog

Admin → Catalog. This is where work types, material types and units are managed.

19

The page loads without jumping

Do this

  1. Open the Catalog screen and watch the first second carefully.
  2. Do the same on Home → Company Tools.

What should happen

You briefly see grey placeholder blocks shaped like the content, which are then replaced by the real thing in the same position.

Flag it if you see the word "Loading…" and then everything jumps down or shifts sideways when the content arrives.

20

Adding, renaming and switching items off

Do this

  1. Add a new item with an obvious test name.
  2. Rename it.
  3. Switch it off, then back on.
  4. Move it up or down the list.
  5. Reload the page after each step.

What should happen

Each change shows immediately, without waiting for a spinner, and it's still correct after the reload.

Flag it if a change looks like it worked but has reverted after you reload. That means it never actually saved.

21

The duplicate-name warning

Do this

  1. Start adding a new item and type a name very close to one that already exists — for example "Brickwrk" if "Brickwork" exists.
  2. Type it at a normal speed, then stop and wait a moment.
  3. Now clear the box completely.

What should happen

A hint about the similar name appears shortly after you stop typing — not flickering on every letter.

It does not stop you saving — it's advice, not a block.

Clearing the box makes the hint go away.

Flag it if the hint stays on screen after you've emptied the box, or if it names a word you're no longer typing.

22

Genuine duplicates are still caught

Do this

  1. Try to add an item with a name that already exists exactly.
  2. Press Save.

What should happen

You're warned it already exists and offered the choice to continue anyway or pick the existing one.

The typing hint above was changed. This check makes sure the real safety net behind it still works.

23

The row menu opens on top of everything

Do this

  1. Scroll the catalog list down slightly, so rows sit under the top strip.
  2. Open the little "⋯" menu on the very first visible row.

What should happen

The menu appears fully on top and you can read and tap every option.

Flag it if part of the menu is hidden behind the strip at the top of the screen. This was a real bug that was fixed — it's worth confirming.

24

Renaming a tool category

Do this

  1. Go to Home → Company Tools and rename one of the tool categories.

What should happen

A proper styled panel opens with the current name already filled in, ready to edit.

Flag it if a plain browser box appears asking you to type the name. That was the very last one of those in the app and it should be gone.

The Admin tab. Several of these were rebuilt to load their data before the page appears.

Admin pages open with data already there

Do this

  1. Visit each in turn: Approvals, Users, Expenses, Analytics.
  2. Watch what appears in the first moment after each one opens.

What should happen

Each page arrives with its content already filled in, rather than showing an empty shell and then filling itself.

26

Approving and declining

Do this

  1. On Approvals, approve one item and decline another.
  2. Do it for each kind you can find: a resource request, a field request, and a transfer.
  3. Reload the page after each action.

What should happen

The item updates the moment you tap, and it's still in the right state after reloading.

This screen was split into three separate pieces. Each piece needs checking on its own.

27

Approve and decline buttons are big enough

Do this

  1. On a phone, look at the approve and decline buttons on the Approvals screen.

What should happen

Both are easy to tap and far enough apart that you're not going to hit the wrong one by accident.

28

Supervisors can't reach admin screens

Do this

  1. Sign in as a Supervisor, not an Admin.
  2. Paste an admin web address straight into the address bar — the Users screen, for example.
  3. Try the same with the Company Tools screen.

What should happen

You're refused — a "not found" page, or sent back to sign in. You should never see admin content, not even for a split second.

Flag it immediately if any admin data appears, even briefly before disappearing. This is the most serious thing on this list.

29

The export menu sits on top

Do this

  1. Go to Admin → Data.
  2. Scroll down a little, then open the export dropdown.
  3. Download one export and open the file.

What should happen

The dropdown appears fully on top of everything, not tucked behind the strip at the top.

The downloaded file opens in Excel and the columns line up with the right headings.

General look and feel

Worth doing last, once you've seen most of the app.

30

Everything is readable

Do this

  1. Go outside, or somewhere bright, and use the app on your phone.
  2. Look particularly at the faint grey placeholder text inside empty boxes, and at the small labels on chips and tags.

What should happen

You can read all of it without squinting or shading the screen.

Some text was made larger and lighter for exactly this reason. Tell us about anything still too faint or too small — it's useful even if it wasn't on the fix list.

31

Pop-up panels behave

Do this

  1. Open any panel that appears over the page — adding a catalog item, a confirmation, a tool's history.
  2. On a computer, press the Esc key.
  3. Open it again and try to scroll the page behind it.
  4. Open it again and tap the dark area outside it.

What should happen

Esc closes it. The page behind stays still while it's open. Tapping outside closes it.

These panels previously had no keyboard way out at all.

When you're done

For anything that failed, tell us three things — that's enough for us to find it:

  • The number from this list.
  • What you saw instead of what was expected. A screenshot beats a description every time.
  • Your phone and browser — "iPhone 13, Safari" is plenty.

Two things worth knowing. First: you can't break anything by looking — but this is the live system, so please only delete things you created yourself during the test. Second: there is no "everything passed" outcome we're hoping for. Finding problems is the point. If something merely feels clumsy or confusing rather than broken, tell us that too — it counts.
